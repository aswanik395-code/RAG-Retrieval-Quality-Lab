# Security Handbook

## Device Security
All company laptops must use full-disk encryption and an automatic screen lock set to five minutes. Personal devices may not be used to store customer data. Employees should never leave a laptop unattended in a vehicle or a public place.

Software may only be installed from the approved catalogue. Requests for new software are submitted through the IT portal and are reviewed within three business days. Browser extensions that read page contents also need approval.

## Lost or Stolen Devices
Lost or stolen devices are one of the most common causes of data incidents, so speed matters. If a company device is lost or stolen, the employee must report it to the Security Desk within one hour of noticing the loss. Reports can be made by phone or through the incident portal, and the device will be remotely wiped. Employees should never wait to see whether the device turns up first.

If the loss happens outside the country, the employee must also notify their manager and file a police report where local law allows. A copy of the police report should be attached to the incident ticket. A replacement device is shipped to the nearest company office or to the employee's hotel.

## Password and Access Rules
Passwords must be at least 14 characters and are rotated every 180 days. Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for every system that holds customer data. Password managers approved by IT may be used to store credentials.

Access to production systems is granted for 30 days at a time and is reviewed by the system owner before renewal. Shared accounts are not allowed on production systems, because every action must be traceable to one person.

## Reporting Phishing
Suspicious emails should be forwarded to the phishing mailbox and then deleted. Employees who click a suspicious link must change their password immediately and inform the Security Desk. The security team publishes a short summary of recent phishing campaigns every month.
